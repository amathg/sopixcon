Commitment

Legal, secure handling from end to end

Managing end-of-life electronic equipment engages the responsibility of the company that holds it. Sopixcon secures that responsibility by relying on a WEEE accreditation, an export authorisation and a compliant customs classification.

What this means in practice

  • WEEE accreditation issued by the DEEC (Senegal's Directorate of the Environment and Classified Establishments)
  • Export authorisations compliant with the Basel Convention
  • HS 8549 customs classification applied to all export flows

Why it matters to you

  • Lawful and secure handling of your end-of-life equipment
  • No regulatory or customs risk for your company
  • Full traceability through to final recovery

Key concepts

The three pillars of compliance

DEEC accreditation

Authorisation issued by Senegal's Direction de l'Environnement et des Établissements Classés for the management of WEEE.

Basel Convention

The international treaty that regulates transboundary movements of hazardous waste and its disposal.

HS code 8549

The Harmonized System (customs nomenclature) heading that designates electrical and electronic waste and scrap.

Frequently asked questions

Regulatory compliance: your questions

Is Sopixcon's WEEE recycling compliant with regulations?

Yes. Sopixcon relies on the DEEC's WEEE accreditation, export authorisation under the Basel Convention and HS 8549 customs compliance. Every item is tracked by serial number.

What does HS 8549 mean?

It is the Harmonized System code used by customs for electrical and electronic waste and scrap. Sopixcon applies it to all export flows.

What is the role of the Basel Convention?

It regulates transboundary movements of hazardous waste. Sopixcon holds the corresponding export authorisations for the recovery of WEEE.
